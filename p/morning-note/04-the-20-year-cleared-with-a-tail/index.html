The 20-year cleared with a tail.

Wednesday's $13 billion 20-year reopening cleared at 5.469%, 0.9 basis point above its last quote before bids closed. The bond already yields more than the 30-year.

As of Oct. 22, 2026 · About a 5-minute read

Front page · Treasury supply

The 20-year sale tailed.

The Treasury sold $13 billion of reopened 20-year bonds on Wednesday, Oct. 21, at a high yield of 5.469%. That was 0.9 basis point above the 5.460% when-issued yield just before bids closed, investingLive reported at 1:03 p.m. ET. A tail that size means the Treasury paid slightly more than the market's own quote to place the full amount. As background from earlier sales of the same bond, Treasury's auction records show its reopening on Sept. 15 cleared at 5.420% and its first sale on Aug. 19 at 5.204%.

investingLive · Treasury auction data

The bidding was uneven. Bids covered the offering 2.73 times, up from 2.57 in September and above the 2.64 average of the prior ten sales, RTTNews reported. Direct bidders took 31.6% of the bonds against a 22.9% average, and indirect bidders took 58.2% against 65.2%. Dealers were left with 10.3%, below their 11.9% average. In investingLive's grading, the sale earned a C.

RTTNews · investingLive

The curve puts the sale in context. At Treasury's 3:30 p.m. quotes on Wednesday, the 20-year par yield was 5.47%, five basis points above the 30-year at 5.42% and 34 above the 10-year at 5.13%. The 20-year has sat above the 30-year all week: 5.53% against 5.48% on Monday, and 5.47% against 5.42% on Tuesday. The 20-year is the hump in the curve, and this issue's reading is that Wednesday's tail is that same discount showing up at the auction.

U.S. Treasury yield curve

The front end did more of the moving. The 2-year rose two basis points to 4.81% and the 10-year one basis point to 5.13%, with the 30-year unchanged, so the 2s10s spread narrowed to 32 basis points from 33. Fed funds effective held at 3.88% on Oct. 20, inside the 3.75% to 4% range the FOMC set with a 12–0 vote on Sept. 16 (background). Reuters, citing CME FedWatch, said markets priced an 88% chance of a December hike.

U.S. Treasury yield curve · New York Fed · Federal Reserve FOMC statement · Reuters via Kitco (gold)

Thursday brings the next test of demand for Treasury supply: $26 billion of new 5-year inflation-protected notes, with competitive bids due at 1 p.m. ET. The backdrop is oil and inflation. Spot Brent was $115.22 on Oct. 20, according to EIA data on FRED, and the September CPI, released Oct. 14 and included here as background, rose 3.7% from a year earlier. The likelier reading is conditional: a clean TIPS sale would point to the 20-year's awkward spot on the curve as the cause of Wednesday's tail, while another tail would suggest the strain is wider.

    The front end led.

    Reuters via Kitco (dollar)

    Oil swings on Iran headlines.

    EIA spot Brent fell to $115.22 on Oct. 20 from $118.82. Reuters said oil rose more than $2 on Wednesday after Iran said it saw little reason to expect progress with Washington.

    EIA via FRED (Brent) · Reuters via Kitco (dollar)

    UK inflation reaches 3.5%.

    The ONS said UK CPI rose 3.5% in the year to September, from 3.1%, led by motor fuels. Reuters said markets nearly fully price a 25-basis-point BoE hike in November.
