Morning Note · Markets No. 06 · Oct. 26, 2026 · All 8 issues

Oil takes the 10-year back to 5%.

Brent settled at $116.72 on Friday and Treasury yields gave back part of Thursday's drop, with the 10-year closing at 5.00%. Inside the 5-year, the inflation part of the yield rose for a second day. The Fed meets Tuesday and Wednesday.

As of Oct. 26, 2026 · About a 5-minute read

Front Page · Rates

Oil came back, and so did 5%.

The 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.00%, from 4.96% on Thursday, on Treasury's 3:30 p.m. par curve. The 2-year rose to 4.69% from 4.65%, and the 30-year to 5.33% from 5.29%. The 2-, 5-, 10- and 30-year each rose 4 basis points, so the 2s10s spread held at 31. The 3-month bill sat still at 4.22%. In one session the 10-year took back about a quarter of Thursday's 17-basis-point fall.

U.S. Treasury

Oil did the pushing. December Brent settled at $116.72 a barrel, up 3.1% on the day and more than 6% on the week, and WTI settled at $103.27, up 3.8%, Bloomberg reported. It pointed to Houthi claims of attacks on the Saudi Red Sea port of Yanbu, a U.S.-Iran war nearing its ninth month, and traders unwilling to stay short over the weekend. Stocks slid with it. The Dow lost 1.2% and the S&P 500 fell 0.5% to 7,623.68, its second weekly loss in a row, the Associated Press reported.

Bloomberg via Rigzone · AP via BNN Bloomberg

The more telling number sits inside the 5-year. Its nominal yield rose 4 basis points to 4.82%, while the 5-year TIPS real yield rose 1, to 2.34%. The gap between them, the breakeven inflation rate, widened to 2.48% from 2.45%. On Thursday, when the nominal 5-year fell 17 basis points, the breakeven still rose, from 2.40%. Yields went down one day and up the next; inflation compensation rose both times. By this issue's reading, that is oil working its way into the price of Treasuries.

U.S. Treasury · U.S. Treasury (real yields)

That is the backdrop for the Fed meeting on Oct. 27-28. The target range has been 3.75% to 4.00% since a quarter-point increase on Sept. 17, and the effective rate printed 3.88% on Thursday. A 2-year at 4.69% sits 69 basis points above the top of that range. The AP reported that Wall Street expects a hold this week and leans heavily toward another increase in December. This meeting carries no new economic projections, and the committee's blackout on policy talk runs until the day after it ends.

Federal Reserve (target range) · New York Fed · U.S. Treasury · AP via BNN Bloomberg · Federal Reserve (FOMC calendar) · Federal Reserve (blackout rule)

Overnight the pressure eased a little. Tokyo's Nikkei 225 slipped 0.8% to 62,189.65 and Seoul's Kospi fell 1.5%, the AP reported. The AP's 3:46 a.m. update had U.S. crude down 1.8% at $101.37 and Brent down 1.4%, with U.S. stock futures edging higher. Treasury sells 2-year notes today, the first of three note auctions this week. If oil keeps slipping, the likelier reading is that the front end gives back Friday's rise first. If Thursday's September PCE comes in near the 3.7% the AP said is expected, the breakeven has room to keep climbing.
